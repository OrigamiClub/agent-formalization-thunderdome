# Agent 02 — Gilbert–Varshamov bound formalization notes

I chose the **combinatorial/existential form** of GVB (the primary form given in the prompt), rather than the linear-code/dimension form or the asymptotic rate-distance form. This states: for alphabet size `q ≥ 2`, block length `n ≥ 1`, target distance `1 ≤ d ≤ n`, and target codebook size `M ≥ 1`, if `M * (∑_{i=0}^{d-2} C(n-1,i)*(q-1)^i) < q^n`, then there exists a `Finset` of codewords `C ⊆ (Fin n → Fin q)` with `|C| = M` such that every two distinct codewords have Hamming distance at least `d`.

Codewords are represented as functions `Fin n → Fin q` (the standard Mathlib encoding of the length-`n` product of a `q`-element alphabet), and Hamming distance uses Mathlib's `hammingDist` from `Mathlib.InformationTheory.Hamming`, which I believe counts coordinates where two such functions disagree. The sum `∑_{i=0}^{d-2} C(n-1,i)*(q-1)^i` — the volume of a Hamming ball of radius `d-2` in the `(n-1)`-dimensional space over an alphabet of `q-1` nonzero offsets — is encoded as `∑ i ∈ Finset.range (d - 1), (n - 1).choose i * (q - 1) ^ i`, since `Finset.range (d-1)` ranges over `i = 0, …, d-2` (d-1 terms), matching the natural-number subtraction convention (when `d = 1` this sum is the empty sum, `0`).

I was not fully certain of the exact Mathlib name/signature for `hammingDist` (it may require an explicit `Fintype`/`DecidableEq` instance argument or live in a namespace I'm misremembering), so that identifier is my best guess and should be double-checked against the actual Mathlib source. No other nonstandard definitions were used; all arithmetic is in `ℕ` with natural subtraction, which is safe here since `q ≥ 2` and `n ≥ 1` guarantee `q - 1 ≥ 1` and `n - 1 ≥ 0` behave as expected.
